Project 03 · Lean / 5S / Flow

Reducing Warehouse Movement Waste Through Layout Improvement

A real operational intervention focused on transportation, motion, space utilization, and material organization.

Situation

While managing a branch warehouse divided between office/counter space on the west side and warehousing operations on the east, I identified a large wooden structure (~10 × 4 × 6 ft) situated in a high-traffic central corridor. Positioned directly between the northern shipping/receiving area and key product shelving rows to the south, this obstruction forced material handlers to take tight, circuitous detours around misplaced welding equipment and non-conforming inventory.

Intervention

The structure was dismantled and removed, and its contents were relocated to standardized, 5S-controlled storage locations. Safe dismantling and extraction required three pieces of heavy machinery and four team members working approximately eight hours each (~32 total labor-hours), permanently clearing the primary North-South transit aisle.

Layout Analysis

A continuous motion study of the warehouse floor revealed severe transportation and motion waste caused by the central bottleneck:

  • Before Intervention: Picking and receiving trips moving south from the northern shipping area were forced into tight detours around the wooden shelf, creating a blind corner, restricted turning radiuses for carts, and inefficient routing to product shelves.
  • After Intervention: Removal of the obstruction opened a direct, straight-line corridor from shipping/receiving to the shelving aisles, expanding clearance, improving visual sightlines, and enabling continuous material flow.
Lean Lens & Waste Reduction
  • Transportation: Eliminated detour travel paths between the northern shipping area and southern product aisles.
  • Motion: Reduced worker steps, awkward material-handling maneuvers, and unnecessary turns.
  • Space: Recovered ~40 sq. ft. of floor footprint and ~240 cu. ft. of volumetric aisle space.
  • 5S & Safety: Cleared misplaced welding gear from high-traffic zones and restored standard tool storage.
Impact & Analytics Model

Using conservative baseline operational metrics for branch picking and stocking frequency, the compounding impact of eliminating the aisle detour demonstrates significant annual savings:

  • Detour Elimination: ~16 ft round-trip detour saved per movement.
  • Daily Movement Volume: ~50 picking/receiving trips per day across 250 operating days/year.
  • Annual Travel Savings: 16 ft × 50 trips/day × 250 days = 200,000 ft/yr (~37.9 miles/year of unnecessary walking eliminated).
  • Labor Productivity Gain: 200,000 ft ÷ 4.4 ft/sec ÷ 3,600 sec/hr ≈ 12.6 labor-hours/year saved in direct transit time.
Key Metrics Monitored
  • Travel distance per material movement
  • Material-handling trips and cycle times
  • Pick and retrieval time per order
  • Handling touches and tight-corner maneuvers
  • Warehouse space utilization & corridor clearance
Important distinction: The approximately 32 labor-hours describe the one-time removal effort. The calculated ~37.9 miles (~12.6 labor-hours) represent an modeled estimate of recurring annual waste reduction gained by eliminating circuitous worker motion.
